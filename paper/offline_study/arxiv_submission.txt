Abstract
An order-level size check can admit a large displayed quantity when it arrives as several small orders. We examine this failure and its repair in a replayable limit-order-book simulator. Six 10-lot orders pass the legacy pre-trade gate and its original detectors in all 40 tested seeds. A cap on total same-side quantity removes the benefit from the model's imbalance response for this probe. Its apparent advantage over the legacy gate on legitimate market making, however, largely reflects when a cancellation throttle activates. In a separate comparison of 40 parameterized probes, responsive and unresponsive markets produce identical event streams under the aggregate gate, with zero wealth differences. Detector tuning exposes another weakness: a 180-second window flags a legitimate maker in 34 of 40 episodes when it re-quotes every 180 seconds, compared with none at a 300-second cadence. The experiments show how order representation and timing can undermine the evaluation of trading-compliance controls. They use fixed scripts in a synthetic market; they neither establish legal compliance nor evaluate LLM trading.
