Abstract
An order-level size limit can admit a large displayed quantity when it is split across small orders. We study the evaluation of this failure and its repair in a scripted limit-order-book simulator. Six 10-lot orders pass the original gate and detectors in all 40 tested seeds. An aggregate cap removes their response-dependent gain at the original settings, but that result depends on the market-response threshold. In a separate cap-threshold grid with the cancellation throttle disabled, the same 20-lot cap yields a mean responsive-minus-unresponsive wealth difference of 0.00 tick-lots at threshold 0.4 and 48.00 at threshold 0.2. Throttle tests over 12-minute and one-hour sessions distinguish persistent lockout from recovery after old submissions leave the window. The full aggregate policy reduces maker volume by 14.1% and 28.2% in two short-session controls and by 75.2% in the one-hour test. Finally, a legitimate maker produces alerts in 34 of 40 episodes when its re-quote cadence meets the detector's 180-second window. These are configuration-dependent regression cases for compliance-control testing. The study uses synthetic prices and fixed trading scripts; it does not estimate real-market detection accuracy or evaluate LLM traders.
